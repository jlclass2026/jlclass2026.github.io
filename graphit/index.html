Tomorrow’s 35-minute lesson

  1. 5 minutes · Make it real. Ask the class which world to explore. One sticky-note vote each. Stack notes from a common baseline.
  2. 7 minutes · Same data, two displays. Enter the totals here. Match a category in the table to its column. A table organises facts in rows and columns; a graph shows amounts visually. Both can answer many of the same questions.
  3. 10 minutes · Catch Bad Bubbles. Every child chooses true or false on a mini-whiteboard, then points to evidence. Reward explaining, not speed. Claims use your current totals.
  4. 5 minutes · Choose a display. Switch to bar or picture graph. Ask what changed and what stayed the same. One picture always means one vote here.
  5. 5–7 minutes · You do. Select Independent check. Ask: How many chose green? How many more chose blue than red? Where do both displays show yellow’s six? Keep answers hidden until children explain.

Watch for: reading the tallest bar without checking its label; counting gridlines instead of intervals; confusing “how many more” with adding. For difference, pair counters and count the leftovers. No need for pie or line graphs for these category votes.
